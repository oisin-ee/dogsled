01 / Direction

Talk to leads.
Forget the machines.

Dogsled is a layer on Herdr for one owner. The owner gives work to leads and steers them. The leads place agents on every connected machine as they see fit.

The owner’s thesis · 27 November 2026

1. As fast as Herdr. Every interaction feels instant. If the portal is slower than a terminal tab, the owner goes back to the terminal.

2. The owner works with leads. A lead is a conversation. Everything else is that lead’s business.

3. One machine. The owner does not know and does not care where a lead or an agent runs. Machines are capacity.

Dogsled refuses to be
  • A fleet dashboardMachines are not what the owner manages.
  • A terminal viewerHerdr is that. Dogsled shows what a lead said and did.
  • A code review toolAgents review and land code. The owner decides direction.
  • A place to tidy upLeftover branches and jobs are a Dogsled failure, not a list.
P1

The click is the result.

The app answers from what it already holds. The network confirms later.

Rule: every navigation paints its full result in the same frame. No request sits between a click and its content.

P2

It arrives before you ask.

An unread thread already has its unread text when you open it.

Rule: the app receives the newest part of every thread as it is written, not only of the open one.

P3

Nothing you saw is taken away.

Content never gives way to a spinner, a banner or an error.

Rule: a lost connection is a small mark after 2 s. The last content stays and stays readable.

P4

A lead is the only address.

The list holds leads. Workers, jobs, branches and files live inside their lead.

Rule: no row without a lead. A record with no lead is removed by the service or becomes one decision.

P5

One machine.

Capacity is one figure: is there room for more work?

Rule: no machine name, pane id or machine chip in any primary view. Names appear in one capacity panel only.

P6

Leads finish their own work.

Landing, review and cleanup belong to agents.

Rule: the owner sees a receipt (“Landed in main”), or one decision when work is stuck. Never a to-do list of branches.

P7

Hands stay on the keys.

Rows keep their place, so the hand learns them.

Rule: every lead and action is in ⌘K. Threads switch with keys. Rows never reorder by state.

03 / Identity

Quiet, and exact.

The mark is three runners of a sled. The console keeps the slate palette of DESIGN.md: no colour changes, so nothing here needs a migration. Colour reports a state and nothing else.

Type

IBM Plex Sans: 13 px for the interface, 15 px for what a lead says, 22 px for a thread title. JetBrains Mono for code, commands, branches and times.

Space and density

A 4 px grid. A thread row is 52 px: a name and one line of what the lead said last. A 48 px bar, a 292 px list, a 640 px reading measure, a 300 px team panel.

Motion

Selection and hover: 0 ms. A panel that opens: 120 ms, ease-out. New text fades in over 150 ms and never moves the line you read. With reduced motion, all are 0 ms.

Four states, four marks

Yellow dot: needs you. Indigo dot and a bold name: unread. Green ring: working. Hollow ring: quiet. A state always has its word next to it in the thread header.

Voice

Receipts, in the owner’s words: “Landed in main”, “Needs your answer”, “Finding room”. No ids, no internal terms such as admission, pane, hold or publication.

Names

A lead has a name of two to four words. Until the name exists, the first words of the owner’s message stand in. A pane id is never a title.

Also considered

Two shells that lost.

A wall of machines

One panel for each machine with its agents. It answers “what runs where”, which the owner does not ask. It breaks P5.

A board of leads by state

Columns for Needs you, Working, Quiet and Done. A lead changes column when its state changes, so no lead keeps a place. It breaks P7.

06 / Decisions

Five decisions for the owner.

  1. Home is the composer.

    Recommended: yes. The other choice is an overview table of leads. It repeats the list.

  2. A row shows the lead’s name and its last words.

    Recommended: yes. Todo counts, model and effort move into the thread. The other choice keeps them on the row.

  3. “Branches to review” becomes a receipt or one decision.

    Recommended: yes. It needs the service to remove records of branches that no longer exist.

  4. Machine names appear only in the capacity panel.

    Recommended: yes. The stricter choice shows no machine name anywhere and gives up the panel in screen H.

  5. The speed budgets in chapter 02.

    Recommended: accept them as the acceptance test of the speed work. Each unit of plans/portal-snappy.md ends with the measurement of its row.
